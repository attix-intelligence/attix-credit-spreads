Why V8A Lost Money in June

Plain-English version  ·  July 3, 2026  ·  No jargon, promise

We ran a strategy called V8A. On paper (in testing) it looked incredible. In real trading it lost money. We spent the day figuring out exactly why. Here is the whole story in normal words.

The one-sentence answer

V8A didn't lose because the market did something weird. It lost because we were betting far too much money on each trade — the tests quietly used tiny bets, and the live version used bets up to 57× larger.

First: the test results were fake-good

When we tested V8A, the computer thought the "fear gauge" (VIX) sat at a calm, flat level every single day for six years. It never actually looked at the real fear gauge. So the test was living in a fantasy calm-market world.

🧪 Think of it like this: we crash-tested a car, but the test track was always perfectly dry and sunny. The report said "totally safe!" — but it never tested rain, ice, or potholes. The real road has all three.

When we fed in the real fear gauge, the amazing test score collapsed:

What we thought (fake test)The real number
Score: 6.16 (excellent)Score: −0.06 (barely breaks even)
Worst dip: −7%Worst dip: −32%

That real number (−0.06) almost perfectly matches how V8A actually did in live trading (−1.20). Mystery half-solved: the test was never real, so of course live didn't match it.

Second: what actually happened in June

Everyone assumed June was a market crash that caught us off guard. It wasn't. We checked the real data:

  • The market only dipped about −4% — a totally ordinary Tuesday-kind of wobble.
  • The fear gauge barely moved (22 out of 80+ in a real panic).
🌊 The real picture: it wasn't a tidal wave. It was a normal little wave at the beach. The problem was we were standing on a wobbly ladder in the water — so a normal wave knocked us over.

Third: the size of the bets was the wobbly ladder

Money risked per trade
Test version (the safe one)about 1.3% of the account
Live version (what we actually ran)about 21% — and up to 75%

So one normal losing moment — which would have cost the test version 1.3% — cost the live account ~21%. That single number matches the real June loss almost exactly. Case closed: it was the bet size, not the market.

What we tried that did NOT help

We tested four popular "safety" ideas. Good news: we now know which are useless before risking real money on them.

  • ✗ Auto-selling when trades dip — actually made it worse. It sold trades that would have bounced back.
  • ✗ Panic-selling everything when fear spikes — the fear gauge never spiked in June, so this would never have triggered. And when forced, it was a disaster.
  • ✗ Skipping trades before big news days — did basically nothing.
  • ✗ Auto-shrinking bets based on recent calm — backfired. It shrank bets after the damage and grew them right before trouble.

✅ What DOES work — the fix

Bet less, and have a hard stop. Specifically:

  • Cut the bet size roughly in half (to ~10% of the account per trade).
  • Add hard "circuit breakers" that actually close positions if losses hit set levels (−4%, then −7%, then −9%).

Result in testing: the worst dip shrank from −17% down to −5% — more than 3× safer — and it never lost more than 5% in any period, including the 2020 COVID crash and the 2022 bear market. The trade-off: about half the profit, in exchange for a strategy that survives.

Bottom line

  • V8A's amazing test score was not real — a bug hid the real risk.
  • June was not a crash — just bet size gone wrong.
  • The live strategy on the account today (EXP-800) is the right one — it stays.
  • If we ever bring V8A back, we now know the exact recipe: smaller bets + real circuit breakers.
