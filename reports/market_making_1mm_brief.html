Market Making with $1,000,000 — Can We Do It?

Feasibility & go-to-market brief · 21 Jun 2026 · Maximus + 3 CC research streams (data audit · equity economics · options MM)

Short answer: Yes, market making is possible to start with $1MM — but not the kind most people picture (quoting SPY all day for rebates). That arena is owned by colocated HFT and is a money-loser for us. The version that works at our size lives in wide-spread instruments: options and crypto. And the single highest-ROI move isn't building a standalone desk at all — it's turning our existing v8a edge into a liquidity-providing strategy, which we can backtest today.

Classic equity/ETF market making (SPY, QQQ) at $1MM, non-colocated. The tick is $0.01 on a ~$600 ETF (0.0017% spread). Liquidity provision on our own v8a flow (options), + a ring-fenced options-MM pilot. We already have a validated VRP/credit-spread edge on SPY/QQQ/XLF/XLI/GLD/SLV. Today we pay the wide option spread on every entry and exit. By posting inside the NBBO instead of crossing it, we capture part of that spread — a multi-percent P&L uplift, near-zero added tail risk, and backtestable now on data we own.
~ Possible, earned
Crypto spot MM (mid-cap pairs) and standalone options MM. Real spreads (30–50bp crypto; 1–5% on illiquid option strikes), no PFOF, no registration barrier, 24/7 at-bats for crypto. But the edge is conditional on execution quality and carries genuine tail risk. Realistic well-run book: ~$300–$1,500/day on $1MM in normal conditions, high variance, with real drawdowns. This is a quant operation, not hot-keys.

1. Is $1MM enough? Yes — capital is not the binding constraint

$1MM is plenty to start. Market making isn't gated by capital; it's gated by three other things, in order:

Spread width
Where the edge lives
Adverse selection
What eats the edge
Execution / latency
Binding only in liquid names
Modeling + ops
Our actual capacity limit

The implication: at $1MM you don't scale by trading more size in one liquid name — you scale by running many small, risk-capped books (15–40 instruments) where spreads are wide enough that adverse selection doesn't erase the edge. Capital per book is tiny; the constraint is how many books our models and risk system can run well.

4. Options MM — real edge, but mind the wrapper

Wide option spreads are the natural home for a small market maker. But there's a sharp insight from the research: an options MM book run net-short is just our v8a VRP edge made continuous, naked, and uncapped — i.e. strictly worse risk for marginally more return. The same "VIX→80 = 100% breach" tail that v8a survives because of defined-risk wings becomes an open-ended blow-up for a naked MM desk.

  • P&L = spread capture + theta − negative-gamma hedging losses − vega MTM − adverse selection. The greeks are the business; you must delta-hedge continuously.
  • Honest expectation: Sharpe ~1.5–3.0 (not v8a's 6.4), base case ~8–20%/yr on $1MM, with a fat left tail.
  • You can't beat wholesalers for the good flow: at $1MM you're a prop/retail account, not a registered MM — no rebates, no membership; the clean retail flow is internalized via PFOF. Your resting quote eats the adversely-selected residual.
  • Niche that fits us: NOT SPX 0DTE. Mid-liquidity ETF options (XLF, XLI, GLD, SLV, IWM) and wide-spread single names — exactly where we already have VRP signal and data.

5. Recommendation — the path that actually starts

Don't build a standalone MM desk first. Convert the edge we already have, prove adverse selection live with tiny capital, then decide on scale.

  1. v8a execution upgrade (now, ~zero added risk). On every v8a entry/exit, post inside the NBBO instead of crossing the spread. We currently pay multi-percent in spread we could be capturing. Backtestable today on EOD data
  2. Ring-fenced inside-the-spread pilot (≤ $100k). On XLF/XLI/SLV — where we have signal — post passive 2-sided quotes purely to measure live adverse selection / markouts. This is the number every model needs and no backtest can give us honestly. Cap risk hard.
  3. Buy the data before believing any MM backtest. For a real options-MM sim we need 1-min / OPRA TAQ quotes on the Tier-1 ETF underliers. Until then, treat hourly-CBOE results as an upper bound, not evidence.
  4. Optional parallel track — crypto spot MM spike. Mid-cap pairs (30–50bp spreads, no PFOF, 24/7). Small allocation, automated, inventory-skew quoting. Best accessible standalone MM, but earn it: at low fee tiers the maker fee can exceed a tight spread — pair selection + volume tiers decide profitability.
  5. Scale by count, not size. If the pilot's measured markouts are favorable, deploy across 15–40 small risk-capped books rather than concentrating. Capacity = modeling/ops bandwidth, not capital.

6. Bottom line

Is $1MM enough to start? Yes. Capital isn't the constraint — spread width, adverse selection, and our modeling/ops bandwidth are.

Can we "crush" liquid equity MM? No. That's an HFT/latency game we lose. Don't play it.

Where we can win: wide-spread liquidity provision — first by upgrading v8a's execution (free money we currently leave on the table), then a measured options/crypto MM pilot. Expect a well-run $1MM MM book to make ~$300–$1,500/day in normal conditions — real, but variance-heavy and tail-exposed, and conditional on execution quality.

First move I'd green-light: backtest the v8a inside-the-NBBO execution upgrade (Step 1). It's the highest ROI, lowest risk, and we can run it this week. Say the word and I'll put CC on it.
